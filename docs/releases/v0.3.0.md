## Clayvoyant 0.3.0

Clay stop-motion films, written as plain text. The clay engine is part of the package: characters
are sculpted from a few lines of description, rigged with hands and a face that acts, filmed in
Blender on twos and assembled into a subtitled, voiced film with one command.

### Start here

```bash
git clone https://github.com/xIBBAx/clayvoyant
cd clayvoyant
python3 -m venv .venv && . .venv/bin/activate      # Windows: .venv\Scripts\activate
pip install -e ".[clay,voices]"
sastudio voices install kokoro                     # a free neural voice, downloaded once (350 MB)
sastudio film examples/the-fast-one --preset draft
```

You need Python 3.11+, FFmpeg and Blender 4.2 or newer. A GPU makes filming much faster; it is not
required. No account and no API key. This version is not on PyPI: install from the repository.

### The sample film

`The_Fast_One.en-GB.mp4`, attached here with its subtitles, is exactly what
`sastudio film examples/the-fast-one --preset preview` makes: 1280x720 at 24 frames a second,
1,974 frames, 82.25 seconds, two scenes, seventeen filmed shots (two of them reactions cut into the
middle of a line), thirteen Kokoro lines at -16 LUFS. Filming every shot from nothing took 55
minutes on a laptop GeForce GTX 1660 Ti (measured on 11 November; the copy attached here was filmed
again on 12 November with solid contact, sharing the machine with another film). Nothing in it
passes through anything: `tools/check_contacts.py` finds 0 intersections in 116 sampled drawings.

### For India

`sastudio init my-film --template india` makes five short scenes about the scams that reach Indian
phones every day, in English and Hindi: a "digital arrest" video call, a UPI request that wants a
PIN, a caller who needs an OTP, an electricity-bill message with an app to install, and a
customer-care number from the top of a search. Every scene names the I4C, RBI or Department of
Telecommunications advisory its advice follows. Lip sync and subtitles work in Hindi and in eight
more Indian scripts. See `docs/INDIA.md`.

### New

- **The clay engine.** Signed-distance sculpting fused by marching cubes, a hand rig with four
  fingers and a thumb, a 21-control face, hair grown over the scalp, a clay material with thumb
  marks. Sets (office, reception, meeting room, home office, cafe, corridor, street, classroom) and
  props are built by code; nothing is loaded from a model, texture or photograph.
- **`sastudio film`**: voices, sculpts, sets, cameras, light, lip sync, subtitles and assembly in
  one command. `sastudio motion` films one silent scene or shot to check the direction.
- **Acting**: walking on planted feet, sitting and standing, forty gestures, twenty-one feelings,
  listeners who react, people who turn to whoever they are talking to, mugs and phones in hand.
- **Direction you can write**: hand-placed cameras; a shot can cover a `part` of a line and the cut
  lands where the speaker's mouth rests; line `beats` (business at a moment inside a line);
  `direction.light` (key, fill, rim, sun, sky, bounce, tint); `direction.props` (laptops close,
  things slide, turn, hide); `direction.dressing` (steam, birds); `direction.clock`;
  `direction.end_with`.
- **Direction you don't have to write**: the planner cuts to the listener inside long lines, gives
  every face room to look into, never shows the back of a speaker's head, keeps everyone a shot is
  on inside the frame, and never opens or ends two scenes the same way.
- **Free voices**: Kokoro (British and American English, Hindi) on your computer, with phoneme
  timings that drive the mouths; or your own recordings. Paid providers need `--live` and a budget.
- **A renderer that cleans up after itself**: a hung Blender is stopped by a watchdog and the shot
  started once more from its first missing drawing; blank drawings are refused; no Blender outlives
  `sastudio`, even after `kill -9` on Linux; `sastudio clean --shots` prunes filmed shots nothing
  uses any more.
- **Imported models**: rigged glTF, FBX or OBJ characters act in clay scenes; sculpted characters
  export to GLB, FBX, OBJ or `.blend`.
- **Solid contact**: feet land on what is under them, bodies rest on seats, hands on tables; chairs
  are pulled back to sit and pushed back to stand; walks go round the furniture; mugs and phones are
  held from the outside. `tools/check_contacts.py` proves a filmed project shot by shot.
- **Nine Indian scripts**: lip sync reads Devanagari, Bengali, Gurmukhi, Gujarati, Odia, Tamil,
  Telugu, Kannada and Malayalam; subtitles and cards are set in each with bundled Noto Sans fonts.
- **`sastudio brief`**: a writing brief any person or chat assistant can write a scene from, read
  from the validator's own tables (`docs/WRITING_WITH_AN_ASSISTANT.md`).

### Measured

On one laptop (Intel Core i7-10750H, GeForce GTX 1660 Ti 6 GB, Blender 5.2.2 under WSL2), November
2026: sculpting the sample's three characters 46 s; voicing its 13 lines with Kokoro 26 s for 47 s
of speech; a drawing at `preview` 4.7 s on average (4.2 to 5.0), at `draft` 2.2 to 2.6 s; the cards
renderer makes the 14-minute starter film in 90 s at `preview` and 2 min 41 s at `final`.

### Known limitations

- The `final` preset (1920x1080) has not been run for a whole film; the sample uses `preview`.
- Kokoro has no Indian-English accent; English is read in a British or American voice.
- Another language is another film: translations are written in the scene files, one render each.
- No sound-effect or music library: a film has its dialogue and, if you add one, a music track.
- Imported models do not sit or use their hands.
- Blender for Windows driven from WSL2 is unverified for this version.
- A clay film on native Windows or macOS has not been run.
